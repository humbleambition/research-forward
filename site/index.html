Key takeaways

What 202 tests say about popular setups.

Costs kill most intraday edges

The best intraday edge found (a stocks-in-play opening-range breakout) makes about +0.1R a trade, and each basis point of slippage costs about 0.06R: 12.8% a year at 1bp, 4.7% at 3bp, negative at 5bp. Buying the open and selling the close earns about 2bp a day against about 3bp of cost.

A high win rate is not an edge

Win rate mostly reflects where the entry sits between stop and target. A fade-to-POC rule won 84% of trades and made +0.007R each, below zero at 3bp. The ORB wins only 32% but makes money, because its winners are 3x its losers.

Chart patterns don't beat random entries

Triangles, wedges, flags, cup & handle: 0 of 892 pass. Their excess over date-matched random entries with the same bracket is about zero. Support bases: 0 of 336. YouTube ICT/sweep/FVG index setups lost in both periods, several 75–100% at 1% risk.

Oscillators fail on 5-min bars; daily RSI2 holds

Book RSI setups (regime switching, double bottoms, divergence) on 5-minute bars: 0 of 35,514 variants pass. Daily RSI2 on SPY (buy RSI2 < 20 above the 200-day, exit RSI2 > 70) made 8.2% a year out-of-sample with an −8.4% drawdown.

In-sample rank barely predicts the future

Ranking variants on 2016–2020 told you little about 2021–2026: rank correlations of 0.25, −0.02, −0.07 and 0.26 across four screens. A result only counts if it holds in both halves.

Famous anomalies have faded

Intraday momentum, turn-of-month, index inclusion and the pre-earnings reversal were strong in their papers' years and mostly gone since. Growth-momentum swing setups did well in 2016–20 and went flat or negative after.

What survives is simple and slow

A weekly QQQ rotation (QQQ above its 200-day, else the strongest of IEF/TLT/GLD/UUP, else BIL) made 24.2% a year with a −25% drawdown, against 20.7% and −35% for buy-and-hold. Paired 50/50 with the ORB: 19.1%, −12.5% drawdown, no losing year.

Methods

Every rule is picked on 2016–2020, then run once, blind, on 2021–2026.

If you try many variants, some look great by luck. The unseen years show which survive.

In-sample / out-of-sample

Settings are chosen on 2016–2020 only (Oct 20 2016 to Dec 31 2020 for daily tests), then tested unchanged on 2021 to Oct 20 2026. Exceptions where data starts later: earnings options (IS 2024-01 to 2025-07), options footprints (IS 2024-02 to 2025-06), short interest (IS 2018–20), prediction markets (earlier vs later half of events).

Multiple testing

Where hundreds of variants or buckets are tested, Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery correction (q < 0.10) decides whether anything is real, not the best-looking variant.

Controls

Signals are compared against random entries matched on date and volatility with the same stop and target, random price levels at the same distance, plain 20-bar breakouts and NR7 bars, and buy-and-hold over the same days.

Design constraints

  • Long-only cash account, max 1x, settled cash (T+1)
  • No margin, no short selling, no leveraged ETFs
  • No averaging down; hard stops
  • Shorts tested separately as CFDs; leverage kept for a later futures study

3.2 ORB intraday (stocks in play)16 tests · 1 pass · 5 weak · 8 fail · 2 inconclusive

The edge is real but thin: about +0.1R a trade, and costs run about 0.06R per bp of slippage. A 0.2 ATR stop with a 3R target is the best balance: 12.8% at 1bp, 4.7–5.7% at 3bp, negative at 5bp. Skipping Tuesdays and monthly OpEx Fridays helps.

  • Partials, trailing stops and overnight holds raise the win rate (to ~39%) but cut return.
  • ETFs have no edge after fees.
  • Fees are a fixed-ish cost per share and per order, so small slots pay more: at $10k (5 slots of about $1k) the ORB sleeve nets about +1.9% a year; at $50k about +10.5%. Fewer slots keep the edge per trade but make the sleeve bumpier (1 slot: −28% worst drawdown vs −16% with 5).
  • The stop-run re-entry (buying back at the old stop) turns the edge negative: trades that come back to the stop are mostly the failing ones.
  • Real fills are the main unknown, which is why the forward test matters.

3.4 Earnings (gaps, PEAD, pre-earnings, options)16 tests · 5 weak · 11 fail

Nothing beats SPY.

  • A pre-earnings run-up predicts a bigger gap but not its direction. The quick gap trade is ~0 after costs out-of-sample, and shorts lose because pre-earnings drift is upward for everyone.
  • PEAD tops out at 12.5% with a −25% DD.
  • Options price the move fairly-to-rich: implied 6–7% vs realized 4.5% median. Realized beats implied only 33–35% of the time, and long straddles lose 8–11% a trade. Short straddles are about break-even with catastrophic tails, and not possible in a cash account.
  • The Quantpedia pre-earnings reversal is gone in this data. Earnings-date shifts and PEAD with high volume show positive books, but the per-event edge is near zero and nothing survives the multiple-testing check. 10-slot books with fixed holds can beat random books by luck of which early reporters they lock onto, so trust the per-event numbers.
